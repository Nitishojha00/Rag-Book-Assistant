The Solar System

The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system made up of the Sun and the objects that orbit it, including eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets. The Sun contains more than 99 percent of the system's total mass, and its gravity keeps everything in orbit around it.

The planets are divided into two groups. The four inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, are rocky and relatively small. The four outer planets, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, are much larger. Jupiter is the largest planet and is mostly made of hydrogen and helium. Saturn is famous for its prominent ring system, which is made mostly of ice particles.

Earth is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface and the only one known to support life. It has a strong magnetic field generated by its molten outer core, which helps shield the surface from charged particles released by the Sun. Earth takes about 365 days to complete one orbit around the Sun, and its axial tilt is responsible for the changing seasons.

Mars is often called the Red Planet because iron oxide on its surface gives it a rusty color. It has the tallest volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons, and a thin atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide. Several robotic missions have explored Mars, searching for evidence that liquid water once flowed on its surface.

Comets are icy bodies that release gas and dust when they approach the Sun, forming a visible coma and sometimes a tail. Most comets come from two distant regions: the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune and the much more distant Oort Cloud. Asteroids are rocky remnants from the early Solar System, and most of them orbit in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
